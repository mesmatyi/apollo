## Overview

An autonomous driving system integrates vehicle engineering, artificial intelligence, computer science, automatic control and other disciplines, and is a very complex system. Building a closed-loop autonomous driving system not only has a high technical threshold, but also faces challenges such as high capital cost and policy constraints. The emergence of the Apollo open-source platform has greatly lowered the technical threshold for learning autonomous driving.

The "On-Vehicle Tutorial" series of documents takes the [Lincoln MKZ] as its subject and describes how to use the Apollo autonomous driving platform for on-vehicle practice.

## Usage Scenarios

This document covers the following Apollo usage scenarios:

- Building record-and-replay autonomous driving
  
  Record-and-replay autonomous driving means letting the vehicle drive autonomously along a pre-recorded trajectory. It involves the most basic drive-by-wire chassis capability, localization capability and control capability in autonomous driving, and is a minimal subset of an autonomous driving system. Record-and-replay autonomous driving is also a prerequisite for closed-campus autonomous driving.

- Building LiDAR-based closed-campus autonomous driving
  
  Before carrying out closed-campus autonomous driving, please first complete record-and-replay autonomous driving by following the documents, to make sure the related software and hardware are installed and configured correctly.

- Building camera-based closed-campus autonomous driving
  
  Before carrying out closed-campus autonomous driving, please first complete record-and-replay autonomous driving by following the documents, to make sure the related software and hardware are installed and configured correctly.

## Prerequisites

- You already have a vehicle and sensors
  
  Please follow the "On-Vehicle Tutorial" series of documents to complete the DBC (Database Can) adaptation, Apollo software installation, sensor integration, configuration and calibration, so as to build a closed-loop autonomous vehicle based on Apollo.

- You do not have a vehicle and sensors
  
  If you do not have a vehicle and sensors, you can purchase them and follow the documents to install and adapt the software and hardware, integrate, configure and calibrate the sensors, and test. Because vehicles for building a closed-loop autonomous driving system involve problems such as high hardware cost, long procurement cycles and limited test sites, you can also choose the [Apollo autonomous driving development kit](https://developer.apollo.auto/apollo_d_kit.html) and the **Fuel autonomous driving R&D cloud** to reduce the R&D cost of each step.

## Record-and-Replay Setup

- Apollo system installation
  
  Before integrating the vehicle, first complete the software and hardware installation of the Apollo system; then complete the software installation of the Hardware 3.0 (HW 3.0) computing unit, including Ubuntu Linux and the Apollo software system.

- Vehicle integration
  
  In the vehicle integration step, you will complete the integration of the HW 3.0 computing unit, router, GNSS/INS device, display and other devices on the vehicle.

- Localization module configuration
  
  Configuration and verification of the GNSS/INS device and the localization module.

- Vehicle dynamics calibration
  
  This step describes how to generate the vehicle dynamics calibration table, forming a control closed loop to obtain better control results. Throttle and brake calibration is a prerequisite for precise longitudinal control of the vehicle. You can use the parameters pre-calibrated by the system, or recalibrate by following the document to obtain better control results.

- Vehicle record-and-replay demonstration
  
  After completing the above software and hardware installation, calibration and system file configuration, you can record the vehicle trajectory and replay it through the Dreamview interface to complete your first record-and-replay demonstration.
