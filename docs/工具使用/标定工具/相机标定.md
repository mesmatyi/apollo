## Overview

The camera extrinsics are precisely calibrated using a calibration board. After calibration, the position and attitude of the camera coordinate system relative to the LiDAR coordinate system can be obtained.


## Module Startup Dependencies


The calibration tool depends on Localization and the gnss, lidar and camera drivers. Before calibration, please make sure the related dependencies are correctly installed and configured.


## Precautions

Calibration environment: to ensure the accuracy and success rate of calibration board recognition, avoid strong light during testing. Try to choose a shaded place, or test in the early morning or evening.


## Procedure

### Step 1: Select the Mode

On the Dreamview+ page, select the **Camera Calibration** mode.

![image.png](https://bce.bdstatic.com/doc/Apollo-Homepage-Document/Apollo_Studio/image_0baa215.png)

### Step 2: Environment Check

1. Select a camera. This section uses the front_12mm camera as an example.

   After the camera is selected, the environment check for collection with the current camera begins.

2. When both the Camera status and the Lidar status are normal, you can proceed to the next step.



### Step 3: Data Collection

#### 1. Fix the calibration board.

  ![image.png](https://bce.bdstatic.com/doc/Apollo-Homepage-Document/Apollo_Studio/image_4687df0.png)

  For the calibration board model selection and purchase links, see [Calibration Board Model Selection](Apollo_Studio/本地工具台/附录.md).

  > Notes:
  > - How to fix the calibration board: adjust the height with a stand so that it is level with the camera, achieving a "floating in mid-air" effect, with no other objects around the edges of the calibration board.
  > - Always use a stand to hold up the calibration board during calibration, and avoid objects with a shape similar to the calibration board in the environment. During testing, there should be no other objects within a 1.5 m radius of the calibration board's position. The attitude of the calibration board is shown in the figure (the calibration board is clamped by the stand at an angle). During collection, make sure the calibration board is completely still, with no shaking.
  > - Use the live image to make sure the whole calibration board is within the field of view of the image.

#### 2. Click "Start Collection"

#### 3. After the image appears in the camera image frame, click "Collect"

  ![a293403e60c04593aa8c90eb3.jpg](https://bce.bdstatic.com/doc/Apollo-Homepage-Document/Apollo_Studio/a293403e60c04593aa8c90eb3_a293403.jpg)

  > Notes:
  > - Using the live point cloud, make sure at least 4 point cloud beams fall on the calibration board. Collect at two different distances from the camera (see the table below for the specific distances), and at each distance place the board at the left edge, the middle and the right edge of the image.
  > - It is recommended to collect at most 20 images; fewer than 1 image cannot be submitted. Considering the collection quality, it is best to collect a full 6 images.

  |  | Fisheye camera | 2.3mm camera | 6mm camera | 12mm camera |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 16-line LiDAR | 1m, 2m | 1.5m, 2.5m | 2.5m, 3.5m | 3m, 5m |
  | 32-line LiDAR | 1m, 2m | 2m, 3.5m | 3m, 4.5m | 4m, 6m |
  | LiDAR with 64 or more lines | 1m, 2m | 2m, 3.5m | 4m, 6m | 5m, 7m |

#### 4. Select the images that were collected normally and click "Keep"

   ![2524532429cb24083ae4d43bf.jpg](https://bce.bdstatic.com/doc/Apollo-Homepage-Document/Apollo_Studio/2524532429cb24083ae4d43bf_2524532.jpg)

   Criteria for evaluating a single image: all points are on the calibration board, the lines are roughly parallel to each other, and the displayed lines are the same as the lines on the calibration board seen in the point cloud. An example is shown in the figure below:

   ![image.png](https://bce.bdstatic.com/doc/Apollo-Homepage-Document/Apollo_Studio/image_560d84f.png)

#### 5. Select 6 images and click "Next"

   ![8cffa7849ba623c40bb2e36f1.jpg](https://bce.bdstatic.com/doc/Apollo-Homepage-Document/Apollo_Studio/8cffa7849ba623c40bb2e36f1_8cffa78.jpg)

   > Note: After collecting six images, it is recommended to run the calibration solve first. If the result is poor, you can go back and continue collecting images.

### Step 4: Extrinsic Calibration

1. View the fusion result of the image and the point cloud.

   The figure below shows the effect of projecting the point cloud onto the 2D image. The colored discrete points represent the point cloud, and different colors represent different distances from the lidar center. The edges of the point cloud can be identified from the color changes, and whether the calibration result is good can be judged by how well the point cloud edges align with the edges in the 2D image. The annotation effect is shown in the figure below:

## FAQ

### 1. Analysis of the Causes of Collection Failure and Solutions
- If "no corners found" is returned, the possible causes are as follows, which you can check one by one:

  - The calibration board is not entirely within the field of view of the image; move the calibration board into the field of view.
  - The calibration board occupies too few pixels in the image; move the calibration board toward the lens.

  - The lighting is too strong; change the angle at which the calibration board is placed, or shield it from the light.

- If "extract rectangle corners failed" is returned, the possible causes are as follows, which you can check one by one:

  - Use the point cloud image to confirm whether the number of lines hitting the calibration board is greater than or equal to 4; if not, move the calibration board forward.

  - Confirm whether there are objects similar to the calibration board in the environment, or other objects around the calibration board; if so, remove them.
